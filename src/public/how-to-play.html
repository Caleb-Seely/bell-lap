Bell-Lap

How to play

Bell-Lap is a daily guessing game for track and field fans. Every day there are five championship finals from the Olympic Games, the World Athletics Championships and the NCAA Division I Championships. For each one you see the event, such as the men's 100 m final in Paris 2024, and make one guess at the winning time or distance.

Play today's finals

Guessing

  • Type a time the way it's shown on a results board: 9.79, 1:41.19 or 2:06:26. Dots work in place of colons.
  • Field events are in metres: 8.34, 6.10, 92.97.
  • You get one guess per final. The Hint button shows a slower finisher's mark from the same race, and costs nothing.

Scoring

Each final is worth up to 1,000 points, so a perfect day is 5,000. The closer you are to the winning mark, the more you score. After each guess you see the real winner, where your mark would have placed in that final, and the full results with the world record (or collegiate record for NCAA finals) going in.

When you finish, you can copy a share line with one coloured square per final.

Modes

  • Standard: the daily five, no clock.
  • Warm-up: multiple choice, four marks to pick from.
  • Bell-Lap: the daily five with 60 seconds on the clock for all of them.
  • Workout: endless finals. Each one adds 15 seconds to the clock, and the run ends when time runs out or a guess misses the split.

Standard, Warm-up and Bell-Lap each have their own five finals every day, and a new set starts at midnight. Once you finish a day you can keep playing practice sets.

The finals

The bank holds about 1,100 finals:

  • Olympic Games: Sydney 2000, Athens 2004, Beijing 2008, London 2012, Rio 2016, Tokyo 2021 and Paris 2024.
  • World Athletics Championships: London 2017, Doha 2019, Eugene 2022, Budapest 2023 and Tokyo 2025.
  • World Athletics Indoor Championships: Portland 2016, Birmingham 2018, Belgrade 2022, Glasgow 2024 and Nanjing 2025.
  • NCAA Division I Indoor and Outdoor Championships, 2016 to 2025.

Every individual event is included, from the 60 m to the marathon, hurdles and steeplechase, and every jump and throw. Relays, race walks and combined events are left out.

Where the results come from

Results come from each event's page on Wikipedia (CC BY-SA 4.0), and each reveal links to its source. They haven't all been checked against official World Athletics results yet.
